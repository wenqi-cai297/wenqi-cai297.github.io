<p>I am a master's student in the <a href="https://www.gsse.u-toyama.ac.jp/?lang=en">Graduate School of Science and Engineering</a> at the <a href="https://www.u-toyama.ac.jp/">University of Toyama</a>, pursuing a Master of Mathematics and Informatics (expected March 2027). I'm interested in representation learning for visual understanding and prediction, particularly how the organization of a representation space shapes what a model can learn and how its features can be used across tasks.</p>
<p>My research spans <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.07476">vision-language dataset distillation</a>, <a href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24286">3D human-object interaction generation</a>, and self-supervised visual representation learning. I am currently studying how relationships among patch representations evolve during training, with a focus on the causes of structural collapse and dense feature degradation. My goal is to learn representations in which heterogeneous modalities complement one another, preserving useful spatial and temporal details while reducing computational overhead.</p>
